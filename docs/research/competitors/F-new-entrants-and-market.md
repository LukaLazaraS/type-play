## Top-line findings

1. **The biggest news is Google Labs Playground (launched 2026-10-07).** You describe a game and get a playable 2D or 3D browser game with multiplayer, a gallery and leaderboards. It runs on Gemini, Nano Banana and Lyria. It is US-only and 18+. Making games is free from a weekly token allowance, with more on Google AI plans. Unity Spark will plug into it later in 2026. It has **no export**: games stay on Google's platform. ([TechCrunch 2026-10-07](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/google-experiments-with-an-ai-powered-gaming-platform/), [9to5Google 2026-10-07](https://9to5google.com/2026/10/07/google-labs-playground/))
2. **The most-funded new consumer player is Aippy (NADA AI, Singapore).** It raised "tens of millions" at a **$250M post-money valuation** and reports about 2M MAU, more than 3M downloads and more than 2M games created (self-reported, [PR Newswire 2026-06-02](https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/aippy-raises-tens-of-millions-of-dollars-at-a-250-million-valuation-to-build-the-future-of-ai-native-interactive-entertainment-302787864.html)).
3. **YC funds this category every batch:** CodeWisp (W26), Playabl.ai (P26), Pops (S26), Fifth Door (F25, $20M seed). Playabl started as a "Lovable for games" builder, then pivoted to a TikTok-style feed of playable games.
4. **Almost every new entrant hosts games in a feed or gallery.** Very few export real store builds. The ones that do: **Max2D** (Android APK/AAB, 2D only), **Riffgame** (iOS app with "source code export" on Pro), **Cyberix3D** (Windows, Android and Web; AI is an assistant, not prompt-to-game) and Cinevva (downloadable web build). TypePlay's Godot plus store-export position is still uncontested at the consumer price point, apart from Summer Engine, GDevelop and Buildbox, which others cover.
5. **The money is in the model layer, not the app layer.** World-model and 3D-generation companies raised billions in 2026: Meshy $400M, Odyssey $310M, Decart $300M, VAST/Tripo $846M disclosed in total. Prompt-to-game app companies raise seed to Series B rounds ($1M-$56M).
6. **Benchmark:** Lovable reached **$600M ARR** on 2026-09-24 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/lovables-annualized-revenue-crosses-600m-as-vibe-coding-takes-off/)). Base44 reached $150M ARR in 2026-05. Replit targets $1B ARR by the end of 2026. No game-specific builder has published any ARR.

### 1d. The five most relevant new competitors

**1. Google Playground (Google Labs). The new reference point for "free and good enough".** Launched on 2026-10-07 at playground.google. You chat to create 2D or 3D, single- or multiplayer browser games. You can start from genre templates (trivia, tower defence, racing; the last two are TypePlay launch genres) and upload images that become art in the game's style. It is built on Gemini, Nano Banana and Lyria with a "custom harness". Games can stay private, go out by link, or be published to an Explore gallery with ratings and Play Games leaderboards. Games are screened for safety ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/07/google-experiments-with-an-ai-powered-gaming-platform/), [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/10/07/google-labs-playground/)). Pricing: playing is free. Making games uses weekly tokens: free accounts are let in from a waitlist in batches, and Google AI plans (Plus at $4.99/mo or higher) raise the limit (per [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/google-launches-playground-as-an-experimental-ai-driven-game-creation-platform/)). Unity Spark will add "professional-level" 3D through the Unity runtime later in 2026. Funding: Google. Users: none disclosed. **Weaknesses:** US-only and 18+. Google calls it an "experiment", and Stadia's shutdown shows that risk. It has **no export**, so the game never leaves Google's gallery. It runs only in the browser, with no native phone build. Players can't make money from their games. Game Developer notes that developers have "grave concerns" about generative AI ([Game Developer 2026-10-06](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/google-debuts-new-ai-game-platform-named-google-playground)). **For TypePlay:** don't compete on "play in the browser for free". Compete on "your game, your store listing, your source".

**2. Aippy (NADA AI, Singapore). The consumer-scale leader among new names.** Founder Evan Yip. It launched in 2025-04 and on 2026-06-02 raised "tens of millions" in its first round, led by Glowill Capital, at a **$250M post-money valuation**. It reports **3M+ downloads, ~2M MAU, 2M+ games created**, daily publishing up 10x since the start of 2026, nearly 50% daily engagement among active users, and 30%+ organic growth. Its strongest markets are the US, UK and Canada, with Gen Z and Gen Alpha users (all self-reported, [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/aippy-raises-tens-of-millions-of-dollars-at-a-250-million-valuation-to-build-the-future-of-ai-native-interactive-entertainment-302787864.html)). The product is an iOS and Android app with a feed, remixing and public links. It makes games, tools, stories and generative art. A community repo says it runs on Claude, built on React with three.js and pixi.js ([GitHub](https://github.com/AippyAI/Aippy), unofficial). Pricing is a "Crystals" currency from $0.99 for 100 to $83.99 for 10,000. It has 25K App Store ratings averaging 4.9 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/aippy-game-maker/id6749073777)). **Weaknesses:** it makes web-tech mini-games and has no export or native build. Reviews mention black screens and lost UI after edits, and one claims an idea was taken. It is built for watching and playing, not for shipping. **For TypePlay:** proof that young audiences will pay through IAP-style credits for making games. Aippy is the TikTok end of the market; TypePlay is the "ship a real app" end.

**3. CodeWisp (YC W26). The closest copy of TypePlay's business model.** Solo founder Elvin Fu has taught game development to "22M+" people on YouTube and built two engines. Based in San Francisco, 3 staff, YC partner Harshita Arora ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/codewisp)). It is a browser editor for 2D, 3D and multiplayer web games: prompt, refine, then optionally use block coding or code. Traction: **2,000+ weekly active creators, 17,000+ games** (self-reported). Pricing: free credits, **Creator $19/mo, Pro $29/mo**, enterprise on request ([FAQ](https://codewisp.ai/faq)). The user picks the model tier, Fast, Standard or Best Quality. That is the same idea as TypePlay's Fast, Smart and Best. Per a search snippet of its FAQ, a new game costs ~500-1,500 credits and an edit ~50-700. **Weaknesses:** browser only, **no export** (a game is a link), the mobile app is play-only, and the team is tiny. YC Tier List rated it "C", noting featured games get only hundreds to low thousands of views ([yctierlist](https://yctierlist.com/w26/codewisp/)). **For TypePlay:** the ~$20/mo credit plan with a user-chosen model is now standard. The difference has to be export and a real engine.

**4. Playabl.ai (YC P26). A builder that turned into a feed.** Founders Hamza Al-Ali, Omar Jarrah and Sanad Kiswani previously ran NeonRain, a modding platform with $1.3M+ in revenue and 5B+ views. Based in San Francisco, ~10 staff ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/playablai), [Founderland 2026-06-02](https://www.founderland.ai/articles/playablais-ai-game-builder-hits-100k-users-with-tiktok-style-mpwgqhb1)). They built an engine over ~6 months in which the agent turns an idea into a structured design and assembles reusable parts: movement, enemies, rules, scoring, levels, UI. The beta reached **100K+ users**. After launching a TikTok-style feed they reported **1M organic plays in 5 days across 3,000 games** ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/QeI-playabl-ai-tiktok-for-user-generated-games)). A later founder post claims 4M plays, 15K games, 40K DAU and 300K WAU (self-reported, seen in a search snippet, unverified). The same post says the "Lovable for games" version earned **six figures in revenue** before the pivot, because "people were treating games like social media content" (founder claim, unverified). They pay video creators $5 per 1,000 views. Pricing had not launched as of 2026-06. **Weaknesses:** no export, revenue not disclosed, and a feed of AI games is unproven. Game designer George Broussard called these platforms a "high concept that can attract VC money and not much else" ([X](https://x.com/georgebsocial/status/2062245860638175614)). **For TypePlay:** evidence that the "Lovable for games" product can earn money, and that users want to share games. A share page and link for every game is cheap insurance.

**5. Max2D (dreamloop.ai). The only consumer prompt-to-game app found that exports store builds.** It is an Android app: you type an idea, the AI generates scenes, characters and logic, and you keep editing in an offline visual-scripting editor on the phone. It **exports a signed AAB/APK for the Play Store** (changelog v2026.07.16, with fixes on 07-29 and 08-28). It claims **1M+ Google Play installs** (self-reported, [max2dgame.com](https://max2dgame.com/download.html)). The editor is free, including export. The user needs their own $25 Google developer account. **Weaknesses:** Android only, 2D only, no iOS, web or desktop, and it is its own small engine rather than Godot or Unity. Its comparison pages are self-promotional ([max2dgame.com/compare](https://max2dgame.com/compare/best-ai-game-makers.html)). **For TypePlay:** proof that people want "prompt to my own Play Store game". TypePlay adds iOS, Steam, 3D and a real engine.

*Also worth watching:* **Fifth Door** ($20M seed, Cruise co-founder Dan Kan, party games, Android "Infinite Arcade"); **Verse8** (3.5M MAU, now distributed through ONE Store); **TapTap Maker** (shows what happens to a store flooded with AI games); **Riffgame** (3D on the phone plus source-code export).

### 2e. How players feel about AI-made games

- **Steam disclosures (FACT):** 7,818 games disclosed gen-AI use by 2025-07-13. - **Backlash examples (FACT):**
  - *Clair Obscur: Expedition 33* lost its Indie Game Awards (Game of the Year and Debut) because AI placeholder textures shipped at launch ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clair_Obscur:_Expedition_33), citing Polygon).

- **What this means for TypePlay (ESTIMATE/judgement):** sell to creators ("make your game"), not to players ("play AI games"). Steam requires AI disclosure, so the export flow should tell users this. Store reviewers and players judge the game, not the tool.

## What this means for TypePlay (short)

1. **Browser play and share is now a commodity** (Google Playground is free or $4.99, and there are a dozen feeds). TypePlay's edge is **Godot native builds for Android, iOS and Steam, plus owning the source**. Lead with that on the landing page.
2. **The price is validated:** CodeWisp charges $19/$29 with a user-picked model tier, and SoonLab $19.90. TypePlay's ~$20 credits with Fast/Smart/Best matches the market.
3. **Export competitors are thin:** Max2D (Android 2D), Riffgame (iOS, source export) and Cyberix3D, plus Summer Engine, GDevelop and Buildbox (covered elsewhere). Watch Google: AI Studio now installs prompt-built Android apps through Play with no developer account (2026-10-13). If Playground gets the same, it becomes a direct threat.
4. **Add a share link and page for every game early.** Playabl's pivot and Aippy's scale show that users treat games as social content.
