# Competitors: AI game builders, full analysis (2026-10-16)

Who else lets a person type a game and get a playable one, how big they are, how long they have
been on the market, how much money they raised and earn, what they charge for one game, where
their games can go, and what TypePlay should take from all of it.

**How this was made.** Six research agents read company sites, docs, terms, pricing pages, app
stores, Discord/YouTube counts, press, funding databases and user reviews on 2026-10-16; traffic
comes from Similarweb's public pages (September 2026 data). Every number in the detailed reports
carries its source link and date. Marks used here: **est.** = our estimate (method given); no mark
= stated by a source. Company claims about their own users are marked "(claim)".

## 1. The short version

1. **The market is real and crowded.** We found ~40 products that turn a prompt into a playable
   game. In the last 3 months the giants arrived: **Google Playground** (launched 2026-10-07,
   free or $4.99/month, US only, 18+), **Unity Spark** (Unity's prompt-to-game editor, waitlist,
   publishes only into Google Playground) and **Roblox Build** (prompt-to-game inside Roblox, alpha
   since 2026-07-28, ~9,000 games made). "Type a game and play it in the browser" is no longer
   special.
2. **Nobody owns "real engine + native store builds + browser, at ~$20".** Every game builder
   either keeps games on its own site (Astrocade, Google, Roblox, Jabali, SEELE free tier, all
   the TikTok-style feeds) or exports web/desktop only (Rosebud, Tesana). The ones that do reach
   the App Store are **app builders** (Rork, Vibecode, a0) or a **desktop tool for developers**
   (Summer Engine). This is TypePlay's lane. Even **Android alone** at launch beats Tesana,
   Rosebud, Astrocade, SEELE and Jabali, none of which makes an APK/AAB.
3. **The direct rival is Tesana** (tesana.com, Los Angeles, 4-5 people, launched Feb 2026).
   Same pitch ("Lovable for games"), $25/$50/$200, markets **Claude Opus 5.5**, claims 350K
   creators and ~10,000 paying users in its first weeks. It **left Godot on 2026-09-28** for
   Three.js because builds averaged ~60 min and scene/export errors broke games. Its games cost
   users **$20-100 each**; it has no Android/iOS export (App Store/Steam only as a paid
   $99/$200 service).
4. **Game builders earn little; app builders earn a lot.** No game builder publishes revenue.
   Our estimates: Rosebud ~$2-4M a year, Tesana ~$2-5M, SEELE ~$1-3M. **Astrocade has the most
   players (10M monthly, claim) and earns $0** (no paid plan). Meanwhile Lovable is at **$600M**
   a year and Rork ~$6-14M+. The money follows users who can *earn* from what they build
   (apps with subscriptions). TypePlay's store export is what moves us toward the Rork side:
   "your game, your store listing, your revenue".
5. **The whole niche is small on the web.** All prompt-to-game sites together get about
   **4.4M visits a month** (Astrocade 2.1M, Rosebud 612K, Tesana 273K, SEELE 263K...); Lovable
   alone gets 29.9M. Audience: **70-80% male, mostly 18-24**, US first, then India, Brazil,
   Vietnam, Indonesia. YouTube is the top social source almost everywhere.
6. **We are the cheapest per game by far.** A Smart game at the recommended credit price costs a
   user ~**$1.33**. Paid rivals: Rosebud ~$8, SEELE ~$9 (fast) to ~$33 (pro), Makko ~$10-20,
   Vibecode ~$15, Tesana $20-100, Summer Engine says "complete games" need its $60 plan, Rork
   needs its **$200** plan for a native store game. Only free platforms (Google, Astrocade,
   Roblox) and Jabali (~$1, no export) are cheaper.
7. **Users everywhere complain about the same five things:** credits burned on the AI's own
   errors, buggy/shallow games, "great until I wanted to ship it", AI slop and clones, and
   ownership (Rosebud's terms say Rosebud "shall own" what prompts generate; SEELE gives
   commercial rights only from $50; Jabali forbids selling outside its site; Gambo shut down
   and **deleted every user's games**). Each one is a TypePlay selling point.
8. **The $20 / $50 plan shape is the market standard**: Makko $20/$50, SEELE $20/$50, Vibecode
   $20/$50, Rork Pro $20, CodeWisp $19/$29, Tesana $25/$50. Makko even sells the same per-message
   Haiku/Sonnet/Opus picker (Sonnet only from $50, Opus from $100).
9. **Correction to the other session's chart:** game builders that reach stores *do* exist, in
   narrow forms: Summer Engine exports Steam/desktop/web (desktop app; mobile needs the user's
   own toolchain), Rork Max and Vibecode go from the browser to the App Store (app builders that
   also make simple games), Tesana sells managed Steam/App Store publishing. Per-game prices there
   were too low for **Tesana ($20-100, not $8-12)** and **Rork (native game needs the $200/month
   plan)**. The claim that holds: *nobody goes from the browser to Android + iOS + Steam from one
   real-engine project at ~$20.*
10. **World models (Google Genie 3 etc.) are not a threat before ~2028**: 60-second sessions,
    720p, cloud GPU cost per minute (est. ~$2.50-3.20/min vs our ~$0.10 once per game), no rules,
    no saves, no store builds. Google itself says Genie "is not a game engine".

## 12. Where TypePlay wins, and how to say it

- **Lead line:** "Type a game. Play it now. Put it on Google Play, the App Store and Steam. It's
  yours." Say only what is live: at launch that is web + Android; iOS and Steam come after launch.
- **Proof points nobody else can say together:** a real engine (Godot) project you can download;
  one project to every store; about a dollar per game; you own it, no revenue share; the agent
  tests every build.
- **Don't claim "one-shot".** Unity's CEO and the press already mock it. Say "iterate until it's
  yours".
- **Launch worldwide.** Google Playground and Unity Spark are US-only and 18+; Roblox Build is in
  three small countries.
- **Don't fight free on simple web games.** Google, Astrocade and Claude/Gemini give those away.
  The paid tier sells export, ownership and depth.
- **Market to people who want a game, not to Godot developers.** Keep Godot in the FAQ ("a real
  engine, you own the project"); don't imply Godot Foundation endorsement.

## 13. Store and portal rules that touch our exports

- **Apple:** apps from "app generation services" are rejected unless submitted by the content
  owner (4.2.6), so games go out under the **user's own** developer account (our plan). Apple
  blocks builder apps that run generated code inside them (2.5.2: Replit, Vibecode in Mar 2026),
  so our future iOS app should not run fresh games inside itself. Spam/copycat rejections rose to
  371K in 2025 (4.3): two "tower defence" games from different users must not look alike.
- **Google Play:** developer verification since 2026-09-30; new personal accounts need a closed
  test with **12 testers for 14 days** before production; 1.75M apps blocked in 2025; "repetitive
  content" rule = same risk as Apple 4.3.
- **Steam:** AI disclosure required for content players see; **30.8% of 2026 releases** carry one
  (25,867 products as of 10-16). One secondary study: disclosed 2026 games average 68% positive
  reviews vs 86% for a comparison group.
- **itch.io:** AI tags enforced; mass-produced AI pages may be hidden from browse.
- **Poki:** curated; games "solely created with AI" may fail; shows prompts/process on request.
  **CrazyGames:** no AI rule; < 50 MB, < 1,500 files.
