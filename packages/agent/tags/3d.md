## Tag: 3D (a 3D world with real models)

- Make the game 3D even where the genre's defaults are 2D: the 3D rules above (a `Node3D` scene,
  models from the library, light, sky, a follow camera), with the genre's goals, controls, feel and
  tests carried over (pixel numbers become metres). The HUD, title and buttons stay 2D on a
  `CanvasLayer`, drawn as the UI rules say.
- **Camera**: one `Camera3D` that frames the action and never loses the player: behind and above a
  runner or a car, above at ~50° for a board or a map. It moves smoothly (lerp) and never ends up
  inside a model.
- **Taps on the world** (a cell, a tile, a tower spot): a ray from the camera (`project_ray_origin()`,
  `project_ray_normal()`) to the ground plane or the thing's collider, never screen positions.
- **By genre** (the player's idea decides where it says more):
  - Runner: three lanes running away from the camera, which sits behind and above the runner;
    swipe or ←/→ moves one lane with a quick slide, tap, Space or ↑ jumps; obstacles and coins come
    down the lanes (move the world, not the runner); props from one themed pack.
  - Puzzle: the board lies on a table or floor seen from above at an angle, every cell the same size
    on screen (an orthographic camera, or a narrow field of view); pieces are models or bevelled
    meshes that differ in shape as well as colour; every move animates as in 2D.
  - Racer: the genre's 3D section (the Kenney Racing Kit).
  - Tower defence: `pack tower-defense-kit` (road tiles, towers, enemies, details): the map on a
    grid of tiles, the camera above at ~50° showing the whole road, towers turning to aim.
  - Platformer: floating blocks and platforms (`pack platformer-kit`), a camera behind and above
    that follows, movement relative to the camera, a round shadow under the hero to judge jumps.
  - Shooter: the play stays on one flat plane seen from above or behind (ships from `pack
    space-kit` or `pack ultimate-spaceships`, a hero from a character pack in an arena).
  - Arcade: the play area as models on a table or floor seen from above at an angle; the play
    itself stays on one plane, as in 2D.
  - Adventure: a small world of a village, paths and nature (`pack fantasy-town-kit`, `pack
    nature-kit`), the camera above and behind the hero at ~50°.
- **Keep it light**: a few hundred meshes at most; reuse meshes and materials, and merge static
  scenery (`MultiMeshInstance3D` or one `ArrayMesh`). The check renders 3D in software, so a heavy
  scene shows up there as a low frame rate.
